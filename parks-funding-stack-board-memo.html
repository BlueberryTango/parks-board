I reviewed thirty-five state, federal, and private funding programs against Hartford City's actual eligibility. Most do not fit. A small number fit unusually well — and three of them combine into a single funding structure that could address the ADA audit findings without a bond issue or a tax increase.

This memo recommends one primary stack, one long-range stack, and a set of small immediate applications. It closes with three specific requests for board action.

The completed ADA audit is the most valuable document the department holds. Federal rules allow architectural barrier removal to be presumed to serve low-and-moderate-income residents without conducting an income survey — the step that stops most small communities before they start. The audit does that work for us.

Stack A — Accessibility (recommended)

Remove ADA barriers across the park system

Apply Jan–Mar 2027
Anchored by a program that funds exactly what the audit identified, at a local share the city can realistically raise from private sources rather than taxpayers.
90%Anchor
CDBG Public FacilitiesOCRA · State

Barrier removal is expressly eligible and presumed to meet the low-to-moderate-income objective. Requires a certified grant administrator and an engineering report.

10%Local share
Ball Brothers Foundation or Blackford County Community FoundationPrivate

Both fund in Blackford County. Ball Brothers grants to government entities directly and requires no match of its own. Ask them to fund the local share specifically.

+Equipment
Reeve Foundation Direct EffectPrivate · $5,000–$24,999

Parks departments apply directly; no nonprofit needed. Funds named items — a hydraulic pool lift, accessible picnic tables, adapted playground equipment. Runs alongside the construction grant rather than competing with it.

+Quick win
AARP Community ChallengePrivate · up to $15,000

Government entities eligible, no match. A 2026 grantee built accessible walkways connecting parking to park pavilions — nearly identical to our findings. Opens January, closes early March.

Land & Water Conservation FundDNR · $250,000–$1,000,000

CDBG, private funds, donated land, or volunteer hoursMixed

Federal funds normally cannot match federal funds — but CDBG carries a specific regulation permitting it to meet cost share on other federal grants, and is named as a permissible LWCF match. LWCF also accepts documented volunteer hours. This must be confirmed with OCRA and DNR before it is relied upon.

Stack C — Immediate, low-cost applications

These require little money and modest effort. Their real purpose is a completed-grant track record, which materially changes how larger funders read the next application.

NOWSept 11
Small Town Big TreesDNR · Community & Urban Forestry

Built specifically for Indiana communities of 10,000 or fewer. Small applicant pool. Closes September 11 — three days after this meeting.

NOVNov 30
Walmart Spark Good Local GrantsPrivate · $250–$5,000

Municipal agencies eligible, no match. No Walmart in Hartford City, so this requires a service-area case to the Marion, Portland, or Muncie store. Low odds, low effort.

ROLLFeb–Nov
Ball Brothers Rapid GrantsPrivate · up to $5,000

Fast turnaround for a single discrete need. Also a way to open the relationship before the larger February application.

Requested board action

  1. Authorize submission of the Small Town Big Trees application.

    Deadline is September 11. Requires direction to staff at this meeting or it is missed for the year.

  2. Direct staff to identify the city's certified grant administrator.

    Every OCRA program is gated behind this credential. If the city has none, procurement can take up to 45 days and must begin in October to be ready for the January cycle.

  3. Authorize exploration of a Friends of Hartford City Parks nonprofit.

    A 501(c)(3) unlocks funders closed to municipalities — including Finish Line Youth Foundation grants of up to $75,000 for grounds and property improvements. No cost to the board beyond filing fees to investigate.
